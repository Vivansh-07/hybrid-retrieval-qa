\section{Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:failures}

Aggregate scores show how often retrieval fails but not why. This section examines the claims on
which the standard pipeline fails most clearly, assigns each failure a cause, and determines
which of these failures the proposed retriever resolves.

\subsection{Selection of Failure Cases}
\label{sec:fail-selection}

The claims were selected by a fixed rule before any was examined. Every test claim receives a
priority
\begin{multline}
\pi(q) = 2\,\bigl(1 - \mathrm{R@10}_{\mathrm{full}}(q)\bigr)
+ 1.5\,\mathbb{1}\bigl[\mathrm{R@10}_{\mathrm{fusion}}(q) > \mathrm{R@10}_{\mathrm{full}}(q)\bigr] \\
+ \bigl|\mathrm{R@10}_{\mathrm{BM25}}(q) - \mathrm{R@10}_{\mathrm{dense}}(q)\bigr|,
\label{eq:priority}
\end{multline}
where ``full'' denotes the standard pipeline with the reranker: the first term favours claims the
pipeline misses, the second claims for which the reranker lost an abstract that fusion had found,
and the third claims on which the two retrievers disagree. The 40 claims with the highest
priority were analysed. The selection deliberately contains the hardest claims rather than a
random sample, so the shares below describe hard failures rather than all errors.

\subsection{Failure Categories}
\label{sec:fail-categories}

Each claim was assigned one of six categories defined in advance: terminology mismatch (the
evidence uses different words), ambiguous claim, missing evidence (the abstract marked as
evidence does not state the claim), conflicting evidence, granularity (right abstract, wrong
passage) and reranker error (fusion found the evidence and the reranker demoted it). The labels
were assigned with AI assistance following these definitions.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\caption{Causes of failure on the 40 hardest test claims of the standard pipeline, and the number
resolved by the proposed retriever (a relevant abstract in its top ten).}
\label{tab:failures}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Category & Claims & Resolved by the proposed retriever \\
\midrule
Missing evidence in the marked abstract & 17 (42\%) & 9 \\
Terminology mismatch & 11 (28\%) & 7 \\
Reranker error & 8 (20\%) & 5 \\
Ambiguous claim & 4 (10\%) & 1 \\
Conflicting evidence, granularity & 0 & -- \\
\midrule
Total & 40 & 22 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:failures} shows the result. The largest group is not a retrieval error: for 17
claims the abstract marked as evidence does not state what the claim says. Three pairs of
contradictory claims share a single marked abstract (for example, ``PPAR-RXRs are inhibited by
PPAR ligands'' and ``PPAR-RXRs can be activated by PPAR ligands''), and that abstract never
mentions PPAR. With judgements made per abstract, such claims cap the score of every retriever.

Terminology mismatch accounts for 11 claims, which share few words with their evidence (median
overlap 0.25). The evidence for ``Aspirin inhibits the production of PGE2'' refers only to COX
inhibitors, so every retriever of the standard pipeline misses it. In the 8 reranker errors,
fusion had the evidence in its top ten and the cross-encoder pushed it out: for ``Ivermectin is
used to treat onchocerciasis'', BM25 ranks the evidence first and fusion third, but the reranker
moves it to seventeenth. No failure was attributed to conflicting evidence or to granularity.

\subsection{Failures Resolved by the Proposed Retriever}
\label{sec:fail-repaired}

The last column of Table~\ref{tab:failures} counts, for each category, the claims for which the
proposed retriever places a relevant abstract in its top ten. It resolves 22 of the 40 failures,
including most of the reranker errors (5 of 8), since it has no reranker, and most of the
terminology mismatches (7 of 11), the paraphrases that fine-tuning teaches the encoder
(Section~\ref{sec:res-overlap}). It also resolves 9 of the 17 claims labelled as missing
evidence, which by definition no retriever should resolve. Either the fine-tuned encoder has
learned regularities in how SciFact's annotators linked claims to abstracts, or some of these
labels are incorrect; this category therefore most requires independent annotation.
